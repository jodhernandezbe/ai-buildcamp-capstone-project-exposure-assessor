## Context

See proposal.md. The draft (`tools/exposure/meteorology.py`, uncommitted work by the user) is kept as the method: Open-Meteo historical archive, one year of hourly `temperature_2m`, `wind_speed_10m`, `wind_direction_10m`, `shortwave_radiation`, `cloud_cover`, `is_day` (this change adds `cloud_cover_low`) (wind in m/s, UTC) -> annual mean wind speed (min 1 m/s), modal 10 degree direction sector, modal Pasquill-Gifford class, annual mean temperature (K), with a screening default of 2.5 m/s, 270 degrees, class D, 293.15 K when anything fails.

Findings from running the project's gates on the draft: `ruff` (E501 line length, B905 `zip` without `strict`), function length (33 and 56 body lines, limit 18), and the old stub tests fail (`test_schema` because of the new `reporting_year` argument; `test_stub_not_implemented` because the tool no longer raises and so made a live request). Pyright and bandit pass.

Other departures from the project rules: no module docstring; a local `Meteorology` type with different fields shadows `tools/schemas.Meteorology` (which `calculate_exposure_metrics` uses); URL, parameters, timeout, thresholds and fallback values hard-coded; a bare `except Exception` hides every failure behind plausible-looking default values; no validation of latitude, longitude or year; hours with a missing `is_day` silently count as night; the hourly lists are zipped without checking they have equal length; a pure rule sits in a tool.

Measured once with the draft's classifier (Fall River, MA area, 2022, the Open-Meteo grid cell 41.72, -71.11): no missing values in 8,760 hours; class frequencies A 5.1, B 16.8, C 19.7, D 25.3, E 23.9, F 9.2 percent; mean wind 3.68 m/s but 15 percent of hours below 2 m/s; 21.8 percent of hours are class E or F with wind below 3 m/s. See the open question about representativeness.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Same inputs and method, in the project's layers, configuration-driven, validated, tested, and with failures that are visible rather than silent.
- One shared retry/backoff implementation for outbound HTTP.
- A result type the exposure tool can rely on, with provenance and warnings.

**Non-Goals:**
- Changing how the year is summarized (single representative values). The representativeness question below is a separate, follow-up change.
- Caching, station data, wind adjustment from 10 m to stack height, or multi-year climatology.
- Other meteorological providers.

## Decisions

**D1. Layers.** `models/stability.py`: `classify_stability(wind_speed, solar_radiation, cloud_cover, is_day) -> str`, table-driven. `models/meteorology.py`: `summarize_hours(...)` over numpy arrays of already-valid hours. `tools/exposure/helpers/openmeteo.py`: build the request from settings, fetch through the shared transport, check the response shape and units, mask invalid hours and count them. `tools/exposure/meteorology.py`: the `@tool` only validates the inputs, calls the helper and the models, converts the temperature mean to K with Pint, and builds the result. `config/meteorology.py`: `MeteorologySettings` and its loader. Dependency direction is unchanged (`tools -> models`, `tools/config`).

**D2. Classification: EPA keys, as tables.** Checking the draft's thresholds (task 0.1) against EPA's *Meteorological Monitoring Guidance for Regulatory Modeling Applications* (EPA-454/R-99-005, section 6.4) showed they are not EPA's: the guidance's method for measured solar radiation, SRDT (Table 6-7), uses at least 925, 675 to 925, 175 to 675 and below 175 W/m^2, with different cells (below 175 W/m^2 is D at every speed). So the daytime key is SRDT, which EPA lists for sites without cloud and ceiling observations; its night key needs a 10 m minus 2 m temperature difference, which the archive does not provide, so nights use Turner's key (Tables 6-4 and 6-6), EPA's benchmark, from total cloud cover: index -1 above 40 percent (4/10), -2 otherwise, giving cloudy F < 1.9, E < 3.4, else D; clear F < 3.4, E < 5.5, else D (m/s; classes 6 and 7 combined as F per EPA regulatory practice). Turner's rule that total overcast with a ceiling below 7,000 ft is D day or night is applied as low cloud cover (clouds below 2 km, about 6,600 ft) of 100 percent. Day and night come from the archive's `is_day` flag (Turner's night starts one hour before sunset; this is an approximation). The keys are module constants in `models/stability.py` with the citation, like the dispersion coefficients. In the measured year, low cloud cover is 100 percent in 838 hours and radiation reaches 925 W/m^2 in 49 hours. Out-of-range inputs raise `ValueError`; the helper never passes them in.

**D3. Summary.** Mean wind speed clamped at the configured minimum; direction binned on the circle (`round(direction / width) * width mod 360`) and the most frequent bin returned (ties: the smallest); most frequent class (ties: first alphabetically); the mean of the temperature in Celsius, converted once to K with Pint afterwards (an offset unit is converted on the mean, not hour by hour). The model states units in every docstring.

**D4. Shared transport.** `EfserviceClient` already holds the throttle, retry, backoff, `Retry-After` and timeout logic. A second service needs the same, and the two copies would have to change together, so the generic part moves to `tools/http.py` (`RetryingClient.get_json(url, params=None) -> object`, taking any settings object with `delay_seconds`, `timeout_seconds`, `max_retries`, `backoff_seconds` and raising `HttpRequestError`). `EfserviceClient` becomes a thin wrapper that requires a list of objects and raises `EfserviceError`; its public behavior and tests do not change. A module at the root of `tools/` is not another tool subfolder, so the layer rule holds. Alternative considered: copy the logic into `tools/exposure/helpers/`; rejected as duplication that would drift.

**D5. Request and response checks.** The query string is built from settings (`latitude`, `longitude`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `hourly`, `wind_speed_unit`, `timezone`) with the coordinates rounded to four decimals (about 10 m), far finer than the grid. The response must be a JSON object with an `hourly` object holding every requested series as lists of equal length, and its `hourly_units` must match what was asked for (m/s, degC, degrees); anything else is an unusable response, because what was asked for is verified. Client errors (other than 429) are not retried (bad parameters will not improve).

**D6. Hour validity.** A boolean mask over the arrays: wind speed, radiation, cloud cover and direction finite and within physical ranges, `is_day` equal to 0 or 1; the temperature is masked separately. Excluded hours are counted into one warning. This replaces the draft's check of four of the six values and its silent treatment of a missing `is_day`.

**D7. Result and renames.** `Meteorology` in `tools/schemas.py`: `wind_speed` (m/s), `wind_direction` (degrees from north, wind blows from), `stability_class`, `ambient_temperature` (K), `source` (for example "Open-Meteo archive 2022" or "screening default"), `warnings`. The draft's `ambient_temperature_k` and `resolution_method` are renamed to follow the project's convention of no unit suffix (`exit_temperature`) and the `source`/`warnings` pair of `StackParameters`. The local type in the tool module is removed.

**D8. Failure policy.** Data and network problems never raise: the tool returns the configured screening default with `source` "screening default" and a warning with the reason, the same approach as the stack tier 2 fallback (a usable, clearly labelled value set). Invalid arguments from the LLM also return the default with a warning and no request. A bad configuration still raises at load.

**D9. Year window.** A year is available when it is an integer from `first_available_year` and its last day is at least `archive_lag_days` before today (today is injected for tests). This stops the tool from requesting future or incomplete years, which the draft would turn into a silent default.

**D9b. Archive facts (task 0.3).** Model pinned with `models=era5` (ERA5, 0.25 degrees, about 25 km, hourly from 1940, published with about a 5-day delay; Open-Meteo recommends ERA5 or ERA5-Land for consistency across years, while its default "best match" mixes ERA5, ERA5-Land and the 9 km IFS from 2017). The API rejects an unknown model with HTTP 400 and dates before 1940-01-01. The last 5 to 6 days were empty when checked, so `archive_lag_days` is 7. Reported units: degC (as `°C`), m/s, degrees (`°`), W/m^2 (`W/m²`), percent; `shortwave_radiation` is the preceding-hour mean global horizontal irradiance, which matches SRDT's total solar radiation. Terms of the free API: non-commercial use only, fewer than 10,000 calls a day, 5,000 an hour and 600 a minute, data under CC BY 4.0 (attribution required).

**D9c. Screening default (task 0.2).** SCREEN3's user's guide says to use an ambient temperature of 293 K when it is not known, so the default temperature is 293 K (the draft had 293.15). No EPA source was found for a single default of 2.5 m/s, 270 degrees and class D; they are kept from the draft and marked as assumptions in the settings. AERSCREEN's default minimum wind speed is 0.5 m/s; the draft's 1.0 m/s minimum is kept and marked as a choice.

**D10. Configuration.** A `[meteorology]` table (D-list in the app-configuration delta); the screening default has its own sub-table. Pint's `degC` and `K` are the only unit names the code needs.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Open question: the representativeness of one mean wind speed and one modal class.] In the measured year the most frequent class (D) covers a quarter of the hours, E and F a third, and the mean wind speed (3.68 m/s) is far above the speed of the many light-wind hours. The pair describes no real hour and is usually less conservative than the stable, low-wind hours that a screening assessment normally includes (SCREEN-style tools evaluate a grid of stability and wind speed combinations and keep the highest result). This change keeps the user's method, but the follow-up should return a bounded set of joint scenarios (stability x wind-speed class, with frequency and the prevailing direction), evaluated one by one like the stack candidates. The new schema keeps that additive.
- [Default values have no cited source.] The draft calls 2.5 m/s, 270 degrees, class D and 293.15 K an "EPA screening fallback"; task 0.2 checks and cites them or replaces them. A default is only acceptable if it is labelled, which this change enforces.
- [Provider terms.] The free Open-Meteo archive tier is, to my knowledge, for non-commercial use with attribution; this must be checked before production use. Each call is one request per facility-year.
- [Model and resolution.] Open-Meteo chooses among reanalysis products by location unless a model is pinned, and the draft labelled the data "era5". Pin and name the model in `source` (task 4.2 checks the request parameter) or word the label generically. The grid is coarse (kilometres), so local terrain and sea breezes are not captured; the description tells the LLM.
- [Wind at 10 m is not wind at stack height.] The dispersion model must adjust it; out of scope here, noted for that tool.
- [Wind direction convention.] The archive reports the direction the wind comes from, which matches the schema; the smoke test checks the unit and plausible range, and a documented known case if one can be found.
- [The offset-unit mean.] Converting the mean temperature once, not every hour, is correct and cheap; a test pins it.
